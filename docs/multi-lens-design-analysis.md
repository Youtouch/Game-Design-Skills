# Multi-lens design analysis

Accepted project direction, 2026-10-04. This document records the user's requirements and our proposed reasoning method. An initial, unvalidated implementation is now available in [design critique](../skills/design-critique/SKILL.md), with optional [perspective synthesis](../skills/design-critique/perspectives.md). This does not establish a comprehensive skill architecture or demonstrated quality gains.

## Purpose

Agents should examine a design through several relevant perspectives, form distinct assessments, then synthesize a defensible design judgment. The skillset should teach how techniques work, when they help, how to apply them, and how to investigate their effects. Document templates alone are insufficient.

Design principles guide judgment. Breaking a convention can serve the intended experience. Reviews must recognize valuable exceptions and avoid turning familiar patterns into mandatory rules.

## Establish the shared brief

Before evaluating, identify the intended experience, audience, play context, development stage, constraints and actual decision under review. Distinguish supplied facts from assumptions. Missing information may require a conditional assessment rather than an invented objective.

Every perspective receives the same brief and evidence. Do not change the design's goals between reviews to make their conclusions agree.

## Select perspectives deliberately

Choose lenses because they illuminate the decision, not to fill a fixed checklist. Candidate perspectives include:

- Agency and meaningful decisions.
- Learning, comprehension and transfer.
- Systems, incentives, balance and emergence.
- Emotion, pacing, fantasy and narrative.
- Accessibility and differing player capabilities.
- Creative expression, novelty and intentional convention-breaking.
- Social interaction, cooperation and competition.
- Spatial relationships, navigation and information.

This is an expandable selection guide, not a canonical lens taxonomy. Scope the number and depth of reviews to the decision. Record important excluded perspectives or gaps. Several lenses that repeat the same assumption do not provide much additional coverage.

## Form separate initial assessments

Each lens produces a concise assessment before receiving other lenses' conclusions:

1. The specific question this perspective investigates.
2. What supports or undermines the intended experience, and for whom.
3. Evidence and a plausible mechanism connecting the design to the predicted effect.
4. Assumptions, uncertainty and an alternative explanation.
5. A relevant exception or counterexample, where one exists.
6. What to preserve, what might change, and what observation could distinguish the alternatives.

An assessment can be positive, negative, mixed or unresolved. Do not force different opinions where evidence warrants agreement. Do not request exhaustive hidden reasoning; ask for decision-relevant explanations and supporting evidence.

For separate reviewers, withhold peer assessments until their initial outputs are saved. A single agent can use staged passes, but must describe them as separate perspectives rather than truly independent reviews. Multiple agents can also share biases, training and evidence. Reviewer agreement is not independent evidence of player experience.

## Synthesize by resolving tradeoffs

Compare findings against the shared brief. Identify agreements, conflicts, common assumptions and missing evidence. Distinguish factual contradictions from different priorities and legitimate player differences.

Do not average lens scores or use majority voting as a substitute for judgment. Explain which considerations matter for this design and why. Overlapping claims should not gain weight merely because several lenses repeat them. Strong contrary evidence should survive synthesis even when only one lens notices it.

For example, hiding information may impair immediate clarity while supporting mystery. The useful question is whether players can form and revise hypotheses, and whether confusion serves the intended experience. Neither maximum clarity nor maximum ambiguity is a universal goal. This is an original illustrative scenario, not a reported playtest result.

The final design take should state:

- What works, for whom, under which conditions, and why.
- What should change and what should be preserved.
- Which tradeoffs are intentional and justified.
- Where perspectives remain in tension or evidence is insufficient.
- What test or observation could change the recommendation.

Use conditional conclusions where appropriate. A logical contradiction may be demonstrable from rules; enjoyment usually requires player evidence. A promising design, a coherent design and an empirically supported experience are different claims.

## Evaluation requirements

Test whether the method improves diagnosis and recommendations on unfamiliar designs. Include misleading consensus, conflicting lenses, intentional ambiguity, asymmetrical roles, accessibility tradeoffs, missing evidence and legitimate rule-breaking.

Compare a direct critique with staged multi-lens analysis under documented conditions. Assess correctness, applicability, evidence use, recognition of tradeoffs, creative usefulness, uncertainty and cost. Do not reward verbosity, lens count or artificial unanimity.

An acceptable synthesis may preserve disagreement or conclude that more evidence is needed. The objective is a useful and revisable design judgment, not an appearance of certainty.
