# Synthesize perspectives when the decision needs them

This is a proposed reasoning aid, not evidence that multiple passes improve critique. Use a direct trace for a narrow established defect. Use multiple questions when a repair affects competing experiences, roles, or audiences.

Select questions because their answers could change the decision: what can happen; what must be learned; what uncertainty is valuable; who can participate; who has agency; what observation could settle a disagreement. Avoid a fixed lens count and expert-persona authority. Note an omitted perspective if it leaves a consequential gap.

Keep the same brief, revision, and evidence for each pass. Record a short initial assessment before comparing conclusions: finding, support, uncertainty, rival interpretation or exception, and implication. In a shared conversation these are staged perspectives with exposure to earlier conclusions. Do not claim independence. If separately authorized reviewers are available, withhold peer verdicts until their first assessments are saved; record any differences in inputs. This file does not authorize launching agents or chats.

Group the resulting claims by issue. Retain the origin, kind of support, shared assumptions, affected goal, and check status. Four repetitions of one assumption remain one untested hypothesis. A developer report, rule proof, direct observation, and proposed mechanism have different evidential force. Evidence about an old revision does not validate its replacement.

Resolve the actual disagreement:

| Disagreement | Useful resolution |
|---|---|
| Fact or arithmetic | Inspect the source or reproduce the trace; retain the correction even if only one pass found it. |
| Competing causal explanations | State different predictions and an observation that distinguishes them. |
| Different priorities | Use the brief's priorities; if absent, give conditional recommendations and identify the choice needed. |
| Different participant needs | Preserve subgroup effects and consider options, role choice, or alternate paths along with their costs. |
| Apparent violation of a principle | Explain who benefits, the accepted cost, and how to recognize unintended failure. |

Do not average perspective scores or count votes. Stop exchanging views when another pass would add neither evidence nor a distinct mechanism. Preserve meaningful dissent without requiring artificial disagreement.

## Synthetic synthesis example

A shared star-map puzzle intends joint discovery. One person reads coordinates while another rotates a stiff dial. An information pass favors showing the target; a social pass wants to preserve mutual guidance; an access pass questions the compulsory grip demand. These are hypotheses about one brief, not three observations.

A defensible proposal preserves private coordinates and joint interpretation, offers an easier way to turn the dial, and observes whether both participants still contribute. Showing the target would remove a different demand: deduction. The strongest concern is that a new control might let one person operate everything. Check contributions, recovery, and participant preference instead of declaring the option inclusive by construction. Added controls also have explanation and production costs.

Report the chosen tradeoff, surviving dissent, and what could reverse the recommendation. Confidence should track support, not the number of generated assessments.
