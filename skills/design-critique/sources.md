# Foundations and evidence limits

These lessons are independently authored explanations with synthetic examples. They draw on a research review completed October 4, 2026. No source text, book scans, complete lens deck, or upstream skill is distributed here. This skill has not been validated with learners or players; method comparisons remain future work.

## Craft foundations

Jesse Schell, *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*, first edition (2008):

- Chapter 2, pp. 10–21: experience, introspection, and essential experience. Treat the designer's imagined response as a hypothesis.
- Chapter 7, pp. 82–95: uncertainty, prototypes, and iteration. Prototype fidelity must represent the disputed mechanism; more iteration is not an exception-free guarantee.
- Chapter 10, pp. 130–150: space, state, information, actions, and rules; pp. 154–169: chance. These provide vocabulary, not a mandatory ontology.
- Chapter 11, pp. 172–199: asymmetry, choices, cooperation, and character. Numerical weights and equality targets require justification for the particular design.
- Chapter 25, pp. 392–401: participants, assistance, observation, and questioning. No universal participant count follows from this guidance.

The review scan-checked two first-edition arithmetic issues: p. 157's overlapping-event wording does not establish independence; the p. 164 eleven-only payoff (+5 on 2/36 outcomes, −1 otherwise) has expectation −2/3. The lessons use independently checked arithmetic. Practitioner advice is not experimental proof.

## Observation and access

Mike Ambinder, *Valve's Approach to Playtesting*, GDC 2009, slides 16–27, 35–39, 64–66 ([slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf)), supplies practical cautions about interpretation and method effects. Its illustrative measurements are not an effect-size study.

Martinez, Froehlich, and Fogarty, *Playing on Hard Mode: Accessibility, Difficulty and Joy in Video Game Adoption for Gamers with Disabilities*, CHI 2024 ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642804)), reports qualitative experiences from thirteen participants. Methods, adaptation findings, and discussion were reviewed. Varied goals and workarounds inform questions about agency and participation, not prevalence estimates or proof that a proposed assist works.

## Why perspective synthesis remains provisional

A bounded local comparison of the earlier and revised skills found no decisive correctness gain from the revision or from reflection/staged perspectives. It used three explicitly scaffolded tasks bundled into one execution per arm, with shared model lineage and no token/cost telemetry. Small wording-sensitive usefulness differences do not establish efficacy. Retain direct critique and optional perspectives; this result establishes neither general superiority nor universal lack of benefit.

Du et al., ICML 2024 ([paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/du24e.html)), reports benefits of model debate on specified reasoning/factual benchmarks. Smit et al., ICML 2024 ([paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html)), reports cost and configuration limitations against stronger baselines. Selected main methods and results were reviewed; these were not game-design or learner studies. Neither proves creative validity or an optimal lens count.

Saving first assessments, preserving source dependencies, and resolving disagreements by type are project proposals. Evaluate them against a strong direct critique and direct reflection, with actual costs recorded. If simpler reasoning works as well, simplify the workflow. Agreement between generated passes is never substitute player evidence.
