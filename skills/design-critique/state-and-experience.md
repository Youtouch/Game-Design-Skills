# Trace state before explaining experience

Use this lesson when a progression failure could arise from either the rules or the player's interpretation. Its distinction between possible action and understood action prevents a communication fix from being mistaken for a rule repair. The teaching and examples here are original; source foundations appear in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Model only what can change the conclusion

Write the goal and a compact state: location, spendable resources, persistent flags, time if relevant, and reset conditions. For each relevant action, name its preconditions and effects. Include repeatability, consumption, recovery, and irreversible outcomes. A floor plan without inventory can conceal a trap.

Trace a successful route and a legal route that might destroy it. A single successful route proves possibility, not that every legal route remains recoverable. If the rules are incomplete, state which missing transition would change the result.

Then inspect information separately:

| Actual rule/state | Available cue | Inference or action required |
|---|---|---|
| What the model establishes | What players can perceive and when | What they must understand or perform |

Hidden state is not automatically defective. Deduction may require uncertainty about an answer while still requiring access to the evidence needed to reason about it.

## Worked example: the tide archive

Synthetic brief: visitors should freely arrange an optional display and still reach the archive. A traveler starts with four charge units. Activating the display spends two. The only archive lift spends three, and the charger is inside the archive. No other actions restore charge.

The direct path leaves one unit after the lift and reaches the archive. The display-first path leaves two before the lift, below its three-unit precondition. It cannot reach the charger. A more visible lift price can inform the choice but cannot restore the lost path.

Three repairs serve different intentions: move charging outside the archive to preserve experimentation; refund the display charge to preserve reversible allocation; use a separate display resource to remove competition. Choose according to the brief instead of calling all three equivalent. An inexpensive-looking repair can still change scarcity or the meaning of the display.

Information remains a separate question after repair: can travelers identify the lift control, predict its cost, and understand whether display activation is optional? A valid trace is not a comprehension test.

**Counterexample:** a traveler who has four units but does not use the lift has not demonstrated this resource trap. They might misunderstand the control, prefer the display, or be exploring.

**Exception:** spending the last usable charge on a memorial could be a deliberate ending in a game about sacrifice. That requires a brief permitting the ending and a coherent way to understand the consequence. Calling it intentional does not satisfy the original promise that optional display use preserves archive access.

## Connect the repair to experience

Write a short causal hypothesis: feature or rule → opportunity to act → player interpretation → intended experience. Mark the unobserved links.

For example, refunding the display charge permits undoing a choice; travelers may interpret this as permission to experiment; that may support unhurried curiosity. Only the first link follows from the rules. A pause near the display might instead reflect confusion. Observe attempted actions and ask afterward what the traveler was trying to do. Avoid asking whether the design produced the emotion you wanted.

For ritual or expression, do not require every action to alter strategy. Repeatedly arranging the same display might be the desired activity. Its value depends on context and participants, not a count of consequential choices.

## Exercise and explained answer

Reduce the archive lift cost to two. What is repaired, and what remains unknown?

The display-first path now has exactly enough charge: four minus two leaves two, then the lift reaches the archive with zero. This establishes that particular route. It does not establish recoverability for unspecified repeat activations, nor that visitors recognize the price or enjoy the visit. If display activation can repeat and spend charge again, add that transition and recheck. A useful next observation concerns actual interpretation rather than rerunning arithmetic as a supposed playtest.
