# Spatial foundations and limits

This October 2026 integration uses the supplied research review. The procedures and examples in [spatial reasoning](spatial-reasoning.md) are independently authored transfers, not measured improvements in learner or player outcomes. Private books, extracts, and renders are not distributed here.

## Book foundation

Jesse Schell, *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*, first edition (2008): chapter 10, pp. 130–135, on functional space; chapter 16, pp. 283–298, on indirect control and information; chapter 19, pp. 329–343, on world spaces, with pp. 333–334 relevant to landmarks. The research reviewed selected passages and individually checked folios, not the entire book in this pass. These are craft arguments; they do not validate cue density, comfort thresholds, or maximum travel times.

## Contrasting developer accounts

- Alex Beachum, [The Intentionality of Wandering](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/the-intentionality-of-wandering), 2016, pathing questions and Brittle Hollow discussion: destination previews and hints addressed reported uninformed route choices in *Outer Wilds*. This motivates inspecting available evidence at forks, without establishing a universal cue strategy or measured improvement rate.
- Alex Beachum, [Demaking Outer Wilds](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/demaking-outer-wilds), 2015, “It's Only A Model” and “From Paper to Processing”: a simplified prototype helped investigate narrative relationships but concentrated information differently from traversal. Use it as a reminder to record omitted mechanisms, not proof that text prototypes predict spatial pacing.
- Adam Robinson-Yu, [Crafting a tiny open world](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/), 2021, exploration and summit passages: wandering and a pause at the peak can be part of the intended experience. Route inefficiency or low execution demand need not be failures.
- Jeriaska's [interview with Alexander Bruce and Robin Arnott](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/interview-navigating-an-em-antichamber-em-of-sound-and-mysteries), 2011, perception-dependent puzzles and audio-revision answers: *Antichamber* supplies a contrasting intent of disorientation, alongside a reported move from antagonistic audio toward reflective soundscapes. This is a pre-release interview, not an independently observed final-build effect.

The research read these firsthand accounts; no fresh gameplay study was conducted. Their transfer to a different design is a hypothesis.

## Spatial craft attribution

[The Metrics of Space: Molecule Design](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-metrics-of-space-molecule-design) is by Nassib Azar. Its graph vocabulary can inform a route model without proving an optimal path arrangement. Luke McMillan's [Tactical Level Design](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-metrics-of-space-tactical-level-design) primarily frames its sightline advice around FPS play; do not apply that ranking universally. His [Four Primary Metrics](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-rational-design-handbook-four-primary-metrics) includes illustrative modeling assumptions, not measured universal difficulty multipliers. These source families are distinct from official Ubisoft/Odyssey lessons, whose full content was not reviewed.

## Participation and evidence

The research also inspected Microsoft's [XAG 103](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103), on additional sensory channels, and [XAG 109](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/109), on revisitable objectives and navigation support. These are practice guidance, not evidence that our proposed controls or cues work. The distinction between an equivalent observation and a solution hint is our teaching adaptation; it is not a requirement that every player preserve one difficulty setting. Check current guidance when using it as an implementation standard.

Separate a conditional path proof, an available cue, an interpretation, and a reported experience. None of these sources calibrates a universal landmark count, safe-gap percentage, intensity score, or best assistance level.
