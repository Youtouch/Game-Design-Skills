# Level Beat Chart

Describe plausible experience sequences and the assumptions behind them. Demand estimates support design discussion; they do not measure emotion or flow. Design only.

## Inputs

- The level brief, intended experience, audience, and relevant abilities or knowledge.
- Duration constraints, if supplied. Otherwise keep timing provisional rather than inventing a standard level length.
- Route/state relationships for nonlinear spaces. Read [spatial reasoning](../level-metrics-sheet/spatial-reasoning.md), especially route pacing and evidence order, when those affect the chart.

## Procedure

1. **Choose useful granularity.** For a linear level, list the actual order. For exploration, attach beats to places, events, and state changes, then trace representative direct, detour, and return routes. Do not impose a fixed beat count or pretend all players encounter one chronology.
2. **Describe each beat.** Record actions, provisional duration, available evidence, and intended experience. Functions can include invitation, practice, challenge, rest, discovery, reorientation, or climax; combine them when useful.
3. **Record demands separately.** Keep threat, execution, and cognitive/information demands visible. Optional rating scales or additive totals need explicit meanings and assumptions. Equal totals can conceal very different demands.
4. **Check learning opportunities.** Separate actual prerequisites from a preferred teaching sequence. Prior familiarity, contextual help, exploration, and coupled discovery can each provide an opportunity. Completion through a bypass does not establish the intended skill.
5. **Shape pacing against intent.** A rising sawtooth is one option. An easy climax can leave attention for narrative; sustained quiet can serve ritual or exploration. State what changes on return, including knowledge, viewpoint, shortcuts, or deliberately repeated travel.
6. **Present the relevant sequences.** Use a table or graph. Calculate transit separately from voluntary stops and observed durations; mark omitted dynamics.

## Review questions

| Concern | Ask before recommending a change |
|---|---|
| Repetition | Does recurrence serve practice, ritual, or reflection, or is unwanted effort a plausible hypothesis? |
| Abrupt demand | Which demand changes, for whom, with what preparation, stakes, and recovery? |
| Sustained pressure | Is the lack of relief intended and compatible with the audience and play context? |
| Learning gap | Does required knowledge lack a plausible prior, contextual, discoverable, or supported learning opportunity? |
| Ending fit | Does the ending fulfill its narrative/experience purpose and needed recovery? Highest difficulty is not required. |
| Route variation | Does another allowed order lose required evidence or change the meaning of a beat? |

Choose thresholds only when useful to the brief and label unvalidated ones. For each consequential concern, give the supporting trace or hypothesis, a preserve/change decision, and an observation that could reverse it.

## Output

| Beat / place / state | Actions and evidence | Duration assumptions | Threat | Execution | Cognitive / information | Intended experience |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For branching or revisited content, add:

| Route / state | Beat sequence | Prerequisites / evidence order | Transit vs stops | Change on return | Pending observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

End with the consequential tradeoffs and next checks, not an automatic list of fixes. A three-minute beach or low-demand finale does not fail because its score stays flat.
